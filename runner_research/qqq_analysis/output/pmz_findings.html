Three premarket hypotheses meet the control test

PM zone = 04:00–09:29 high/low · morning = 09:30–10:30 · every claim vs a matched null
H1 · morning always tests the PM zone
TRUE — BUT NOT SPECIAL
touched 85.5% of mornings… exactly as often as any level equally close to the open
H2 · gap + PM extension → fade the morning
REFUTED

HALF RIGHT

01 · H1 — the control that decides it

PM edges sit exactly on the "any level" curve

Line = probability the first hour reaches any price level at distance d from the open (the null). Dots = actual touch rate of the PM edge, binned by its distance. If the zone had magnet or barrier power, the dots would sit off the line. They don't — distance explains everything.

02 · H2 — gap × premarket extension vs the morning

03 · H3 — zone width vs breakout quality

Tight zones break constantly — and just as falsely as wide ones

Left bar = % of mornings that close beyond a PM edge. Right bar = % of those breaks back inside the zone within the hour. The ~85% failure rate is a constant of the market, not a property of tight zones.

⚠ The keeper: ≈85% of first-hour PM-edge breakouts fail regardless of zone width — same family as the OR30 finding (91% of opening-range breaks return by 11:00). Never chase a morning breakout of a nearby level on QQQ; and the inverse (fading it) is the family that already failed OOS after costs. The only validated morning information remains whether the early low holds — not where the PM levels sit.
